# Recruitment research edge cases

## Directory and scope

- Empty city field with a target city in the title: include only after recording the ambiguity and verifying the detail page.
- Mixed cohorts or mixed internship/full-time listings: classify each row; do not let a total count containing other cohorts stand for the target cohort.
- A keyword result is not a full catalog. Use explicit “non-full” wording until pagination/API coverage is complete.
- A detail page stored in an attachment is still a JD source; extract the attachment or record it as unreadable rather than replacing it with a summary.

## Rules and quotas

- Conflicting official quota statements: retain both statements, identify the pages/parameters, and ask for verification before a scarce-slot decision.
- Separate brands or hiring entities: verify whether quotas and candidate records are shared; a different domain does not prove independence.
- “First preference gets priority” may describe scheduling priority, not an exclusive sequential pipeline. Preserve the exact semantics.
- A published month or phase window is not a confirmed day/time. Do not fabricate precision.
- Interview mode (online/offline) is a real constraint and must be checked alongside quota and deadline.

## Hard requirements

- Treat words such as “must”, “required”, “only”, degree gates, cohort windows and experience years as hard requirements unless the official text clearly qualifies them.
- “Preferred”, “plus” and “bonus” are not automatically hard exclusions.
- Do not infer a language score, GPA, degree type or graduation date from an incomplete profile.
- A robotics/embodied-AI mention in a general software JD is not enough to classify the entire company; classify by duties, location and the candidate’s current policy.

## Candidate evidence

- Distinguish direct evidence, adjacent evidence, unverified claim and future learning plan.
- Keep AI-assisted or partially completed work within the candidate’s documented ownership boundary.
- Do not convert a project’s dependency list into a claim that the candidate mastered every dependency.
